Distinguishing real clinical alarms from false alarms

Patient monitors raise alarms when a signal looks concerning. This assignment explored whether patterns in the recorded signals could help tell real alarms from false ones.

Explore source ↗

Turning signals into measurements

The notebook works with 5,037 alarm events. It extracts summary measurements—such as average, variation, minimum and maximum—from ECG and other patient-monitoring waveforms. These measurements become the inputs to a classifier.

Comparing approaches

The study compares logistic regression, a simple baseline, with a random forest, which can capture more complex combinations of measurements. It also explores methods for filling missing values and balancing the examples used during training.

Recorded signals → Summary measurements → Missing-data handling → Model comparison → Alarm classification

What the analysis showed

Random forests performed better than the logistic-regression baseline in the notebook’s comparisons. Feature selection and importance analysis highlighted measurements from ECG leads V and II, with other signals providing additional information.

The evaluation uses F1 alongside other measures because counting correct predictions alone can be misleading when one alarm type is much more common. F1 balances precision—how often a flagged alarm is real—and recall—how many real alarms are detected.

Course context

Completed as supporting coursework for Applied Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence (CM2011) at KTH. This is an exploratory classification assignment, not a deployed alarm-management system.
