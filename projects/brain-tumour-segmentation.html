SUPPORTING PROJECT / FUSEMACHINES AI FELLOWSHIP / 2024

Brain tumour segmentation from multimodal MRI

Connecting medical-imaging concepts with a complete deep-learning segmentation workflow.

Read project report ↗

The task

Our fellowship team used a 3D U-Net to delineate brain tumour regions in BraTS2021 MRI data. The workflow combined T1, contrast-enhanced T1, T2 and FLAIR images, whose complementary contrasts provide different information about anatomy and abnormal tissue.

My contribution

I contributed the literature review, analysis of MRI modalities and data augmentation research. This involved understanding the information each modality contributes and examining approaches used in brain tumour segmentation.

From images to evaluation

The team pipeline stacked the MRI modalities, standardized orientation, resized and normalized the inputs, then trained a 3D U-Net using Dice loss. Validation and experiment logging connected model predictions with the ground-truth segmentation masks.

Multimodal MRI → Preprocessing → 3D U-Net → Predicted mask → Dice evaluation

Team result

The baseline achieved approximately 0.72 mean Dice. This is the result of the team implementation described in the report. The report also discusses synthetic-image generation as a future direction, rather than an established result.

What this work established

This project grounded my understanding of medical AI pipelines: how input preparation, model training and evaluation depend on one another. It also connected my biomedical engineering background with the practical interpretation of MRI data and segmentation outputs.
