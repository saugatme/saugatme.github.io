Privacy and predictive utility across hospitals

A coursework study of readmission prediction with a contribution focused on clinical data preprocessing.

Read study report ↗

The question

Can a small hospital benefit from a shared prediction model, and how does differential privacy affect the trade-off between utility and privacy? The study explored this using the Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset and ten simulated hospital clients.

My contribution: preparing the data

I contributed to preprocessing the clinical data. The report describes cohort construction, missing-data handling, diagnosis and medication features, encoding and scaling before the model comparisons.

The study retains one encounter per patient to reduce overlap between training and evaluation data, excludes terminal-care and hospice discharge dispositions, and defines readmission within 30 days as the prediction target.

Study findings

The non-private global model achieved small-hospital holdout AUROC of 0.626 in the random partition and 0.639 in the age-shifted partition, averaged across five seeds. Receiving that model was more useful than training on the small hospital’s 400 local records alone.

The loss-based membership-inference attack was already near chance without differential privacy. The experiment therefore could not identify a privacy budget at which a measurable attack advantage disappeared. These are findings of the overall study; my contribution was the preprocessing work.
