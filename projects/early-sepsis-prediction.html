Forecasting sepsis severity from clinical time series

A custom neural network combining clinical measurements with temporal trends at three forecast horizons.

The task

This assignment explored prediction of the dataset’s five-level severity label and binary sepsis status two, four and six hours ahead. It connected clinical feature preparation with neural-network design and evaluation.

Implementation

The notebook implements a shared 51 → 64 → 32 backbone with two output heads: five severity classes and a binary probability. The 51 inputs combine 43 clinical variables with eight one- and two-hour trend features for lactate, mean arterial pressure, arterial pH and procalcitonin.

Preprocessing uses within-patient forward filling and imputation. Training uses PyTorch, class-weighted severity loss, an auxiliary binary loss, dropout and early stopping. The report describes a custom network, training loop and evaluation workflow rather than a pre-built predictive model.

Looking beyond a single score

Permutation importance examines which variables affect AUROC, while a feature-ablation experiment explores how many ranked inputs retain 95% of the baseline AUROC. Heart time volume, C-reactive protein and bilirubin rank prominently in the saved results.
